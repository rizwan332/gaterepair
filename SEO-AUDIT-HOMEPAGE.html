The short version

This is an unusually well-built page. The technical layer — canonical, robots, sitemap, redirects, image pipeline, font strategy, video facades — is better than almost anything in this market, and the body copy is genuinely original rather than templated. The score is held down by four things, all of which sit in the first 200 pixels or in the invisible layer around the page.

The H1 is the single biggest loss. “Professional Gate Repair & Automatic Gate Services” names no city, no brand, and leads on the weakest of the eight terms being targeted. The <title> is correctly geo-targeted; the H1 is not, so the page's strongest on-page signal disagrees with its second-strongest.

The meta description promises something the site no longer does. It ends in “Published prices.” — but /pricing was retired and 301s to /contact. That is a SERP promise the landing page cannot keep.

The structured data is one node deep on a page carrying 21 videos, 8 services and 190 cities. And the local entity is unverifiable — no address, no Google Business Profile link, no review signal — which is the difference between appearing in the local pack and not.

Keyword targeting

Primary keyword and intent

The page is currently targeting two different things at once. The title targets gate repair Dallas–Fort Worth — correct. The H1 targets automatic gate services — the weakest phrase on the list, vague enough that it attracts informational and supplier traffic rather than someone with a gate stuck open.

Intent for the head term is local transactional, frequently urgent. Someone searching “gate repair” in DFW is usually blocked in or out of their own property. That intent wants a phone number, a service area, an arrival expectation and proof — in that order. The page delivers all four, but the H1 spends its keyword budget on a phrase nobody in that state of mind types.

Findings

Sixteen findings, ordered by severity. Each carries what is wrong, why it matters, and the exact fix.

CriticalC-01Needs client verification

The page claims to be a local business but carries nothing a search engine can verify

What is wrong
The HomeAndConstructionBusiness node has no address, no sameAs, no aggregateRating and no geo. All four are suppressed correctly by the codebase's own honesty guard — business.address, business.rating, business.googleReviewUrl and all three social handles are marked unconfirmed — so this is a data gap, not a code defect.
Why it matters
For a local service business the local pack is the channel, not a bonus. Without a resolvable location, a linked Google Business Profile, or a single review signal, the page has no way to be associated with the entity that would rank in it. Everything else in this audit is worth less than closing this gap.
Exact fix
This is a client conversation, not a code change. Obtain, in order: (1) the GBP listing URL and the “Ask for reviews” short link; (2) the business address, or a written confirmation that it is a service-area business with a hidden address; (3) the current Google rating and review count; (4) the YouTube channel URL — the site already hosts 21 videos from it. Then set confirmed: true on each fact in content/business.ts; every render site and schema builder already guards on them and will light up automatically.
Do not
Do not invent an address or a rating to fill the schema. Emitting aggregateRating over reviews that do not exist is a structured-data violation that earns manual actions. The existing guard is right.
CriticalC-02

The meta description advertises published prices; the pricing page was retired

What is wrong
The description ends “Published prices.” — but /pricing was retired and now 301s to /contact, and no price list exists anywhere on the site.
// app/page.tsx — rendered description tail …Eagle and Ramset operators. Open 24/7. Published prices. // next.config.ts — the page it promises { source: '/pricing', destination: '/contact', permanent: true }
Why it matters
It is the one line in the SERP snippet a price-sensitive searcher will act on. They click, find no prices, and bounce — which teaches Google the result did not satisfy the query. It is also simply not true, on a site whose entire positioning is that it tells customers the truth.
Exact fix
Remove the claim and use the reclaimed characters to bring the description under 160. Replacement in section B below.
HighH-01

The H1 has no location and leads on the weakest target term

What is wrong
Professional Gate Repair & Automatic Gate Services opens on an unfalsifiable adjective, names no city, and gives its emphasis — the gold-gradient span — to “Automatic Gate Services”, the one phrase of the eight with no clear commercial intent behind it. The geo lives in the sub-headline, which is a <p>.
Why it matters
The H1 is the strongest on-page relevance signal after the title, and here the two disagree: the title says “Dallas–Fort Worth”, the H1 says nothing about where this company works. “Professional” is also the exact register the site's own component comments criticise competitors for (“unfalsifiable adjectives … which is exactly why they do not work”) — the hero is doing the thing the rest of the page was built to avoid.
Exact fix
Replace the H1 and rewrite the sub-headline so it stops repeating the geo. In components/sections/hero.tsx:
- Professional Gate Repair & <span>Automatic Gate Services</span> + Automatic Gate Repair in <span>Dallas–Fort Worth</span> // sub-headline — drop the now-duplicated geo, keep the proof - Residential & Commercial Gate Repair Throughout Dallas–Fort Worth + Residential, commercial, HOA and industrial — most repairs finished on the first visit

The gold span still lands on the most valuable words, and the line is 42 characters — it holds at the current type scale on a 375px viewport.

HighH-02

Meta description is 186 characters and truncates mid-brand-list

What is wrong
186 characters against a ~155–160 display budget. Google will cut it around “…Elite, Viking, Eag…”, so the truncation lands inside the brand list and the 24/7 availability — the strongest conversion line — never renders.
Why it matters
Description text does not affect ranking, but it is most of what determines the click. Losing “Open 24/7” on a query set that skews toward emergencies loses the click to whoever kept theirs.
Exact fix
Replace with the 157-character version in section B. Apply it in both app/page.tsx and the app/layout.tsx default, which currently hold two different strings.
HighH-03

One schema node on a page carrying 21 videos, 8 services and 190 cities

What is wrong
The homepage emits only the layout's HomeAndConstructionBusiness. There is no WebPage, no Service or hasOfferCatalog, and — most costly — no VideoObject for any of the 21 testimonial videos in the carousel.
// JSON-LD types found in the compiled homepage 1 × HomeAndConstructionBusiness 1 × AdministrativeArea (nested) 1 × OpeningHoursSpecification (nested) 0 × VideoObject — over 21 embedded videos
Why it matters
lib/schema.ts already contains a working videoSchema() builder, and it is wired into service, brand and project pages — but not the homepage, which is the page with the most videos on it. The file's own comment notes that no DFW competitor has any detectable schema and video rich results are uncontested here. This is a built asset left unplugged on the highest-priority URL.
Exact fix
Emit a small graph on the homepage: a WebPage node keyed to the business, an ItemList of VideoObject for the carousel, and a hasOfferCatalog listing the eight services. Full plan in section J.
HighH-04

/emergency is orphaned — the homepage, header and footer all fail to link it

What is wrong
Zero occurrences of href="/emergency" in the compiled homepage. It is not in the header nav and not in the footer. The only file that links it is components/sections/landing-page.tsx — so the page's sole internal inbound links come from the eight Google Ads landing pages.
// links to /emergency, whole codebase components/sections/landing-page.tsx:306 href="/emergency" // header nav: absent · footer: absent · homepage: absent // meanwhile, in app/sitemap.ts { path: '/emergency', priority: 0.95 } ← second only to the homepage
Why it matters
The sitemap declares this the second most important page on the site, and the internal link graph declares it nearly worthless. Google weighs the link graph far more heavily than sitemap priority. “Emergency gate repair” is also the highest-urgency, highest-converting term in the set, and the homepage — which ranks for the emergency-adjacent traffic — passes it no equity at all.
Exact fix
Three links. Add /emergency to the header nav; add it to the footer's Services column; and point the homepage's ClosingCTA heading at it — that section is already headed “Gate Not Working Right Now?”, which is the emergency query stated as a sentence, and it currently links only to /contact.
HighH-05

The LCP image is not preloaded; the header logo is preloaded instead

What is wrong
The only <link rel="preload" as="image"> in the head points at /brand/logo-dark.webp. The hero photograph — the actual LCP element — is rendered by the custom ResponsiveImage component as a raw <picture> served from CloudFront, so Next has no knowledge of it and emits no preload for it.
// compiled <head> — the only image preload <link rel="preload" as="image" imageSrcSet="/_next/image?url=%2Fbrand%2Flogo-dark.webp…"> // the actual LCP element — discovered only after HTML parse <picture><source type="image/avif" srcset="https://…cloudfront.net/…">…
Why it matters
The hero is correctly marked fetchPriority="high", but priority only applies once the browser has found the element. A small logo is occupying the one preload slot and competing for connection and bandwidth with the image LCP is measured on. On a cold 4G connection this is typically several hundred milliseconds of LCP, entirely recoverable.
Exact fix
Emit a matching preload for the hero from the homepage, and drop priority from the header logo so it stops claiming the slot:
// app/page.tsx — mirror the <picture>'s AVIF source exactly <link rel="preload" as="image" type="image/avif" imageSrcSet={hero.widths.map(w => `${cdn(`${hero.src}-${w}.avif`)} ${w}w`).join(', ')} imageSizes="100vw" fetchPriority="high" /> // the srcset and sizes must match the rendered <source> character for character, // or the browser downloads the image twice.

Add <link rel="preconnect"> to the CloudFront host at the same time — the LCP image is on a third origin and currently pays full DNS + TLS before its first byte.

MediumM-03

No question-and-answer content anywhere on the strongest page

What is wrong
The homepage has 13 H2s and not one is a question. Every FAQ on the site lives on /faq, service pages and brand pages.
Why it matters
Question-shaped headings followed by a direct 40–60 word answer are the extraction unit for People Also Ask, paragraph snippets and AI Overviews. The homepage is the page with the most authority to win them and it presents no extractable answer. Note this is about content, not markup — since Google's 2023 restriction, FAQPage rich results are effectively limited to government and health sites, so the schema is worth adding for entity understanding but should not be expected to produce visible rich results.
Exact fix
Add a six-question FAQ section above ClosingCTA, reusing entries already written in content/general-faqs.ts so nothing is duplicated into a new file. Questions in section H. Render it in plain <details> markup as /faq already does, so the answers are in the DOM at render time.
MediumM-04

An absolute photography claim the repository's own notes contradict

Needs client verification
What is wrong
The Featured Work section states: “Every photograph on this site is our own technicians on our own jobs. None of it is stock, and none of it is generated.” Two files in this repository qualify that. content/alt-text.ts records that at least one image (faac-01) is a manufacturer product shot rather than a job photo, and that the wider source library shows Southern California work — palm trees, Spanish tile, a Santa Monica security sign — not DFW.
Why it matters
This is an E-E-A-T claim, not a marketing flourish, and it is stated in absolute terms on the page that carries the most trust weight. A single verifiably-stock image makes the sentence false and undermines every other claim beside it. The three photographs in that specific section are client-supplied and confirmed — the problem is the word “site”, which scopes the claim to 122 images the sentence cannot cover.
Exact fix
Narrow the claim to what is provably true of the images in view:
- Every photograph on this site is our own technicians on our own jobs. - None of it is stock, and none of it is generated. + These are our own technicians on our own jobs — photographed on site, + not staged and not generated.

Then confirm with the client which library images are DFW work before any of them appear in a geo-claiming context. MEDIA-PROVENANCE.md is the file to settle this against.

MediumM-06

GTM is the first thing in the head, ahead of everything that paints

What is wrong
The GTM loader is inlined as the first element of <head>, and the container fires both GA4 and Google Ads. The loader itself is ~500 bytes and non-blocking, but gtm.js and its two child tags land on the main thread during the critical window.
Why it matters
This is a deliberate, documented trade — the code comment explains that deferring it risks losing early Ads conversion events, which is a real business cost. So this is a measurement note, not a defect. But GTM is now the single largest third-party cost on an otherwise near-optimal page, and it lands on INP and TBT rather than LCP.
Exact fix
Keep the loader where it is. Instead: add <link rel="preconnect" href="https://www.googletagmanager.com"> above it, and audit the container itself for tags that can move to a DOM Ready or Window Loaded trigger. Measure before and after with field data in Search Console rather than lab scores.

Seven of eight Ads landing pages still render the brand name twice

What is wrong
The most recent commit removed the duplicated brand from 14 page titles, but did not touch content/landing-pages.ts. Those titles still hardcode the brand while landingMetadata() returns a plain string, so the root layout's '%s | Shield Gate Repair' template appends it a second time.
Apollo Gate Opener Repair Dallas–Fort Worth | Shield Gate Repair | Shield Gate Repair 87 ch DoorKing (DKS) Repair Dallas–Fort Worth | Shield Gate Repair | Shield Gate Repair 83 ch Elite Gate Repair Dallas–Fort Worth | Shield Gate Repair | Shield Gate Repair 79 ch
Why it matters

LowL-02

Sitemap priority and changefreq are being maintained for nothing

What is wrong
app/sitemap.ts carries a deliberate priority tier system — contested vs uncontested brands, Tier 1 vs Tier 3 cities. Google has stated for years that it ignores both priority and changefreq.
Why it matters
Harmless output, but it is misleading as a planning artefact: H-04 exists partly because /emergency's 0.95 priority reads as though the page is being promoted when nothing is actually promoting it.
Exact fix
Leave the values — removing them changes nothing. Instead treat the tiering as what it is: a to-do list for the internal link graph, which is read. Anything above 0.85 in that file should be reachable from the homepage or the header.
LowL-03

robots.txt Host directive is Yandex-only

What is wrong
app/robots.ts emits host: business.url. Google has never supported the Host directive; only Yandex reads it.
Why it matters
It does no harm and is ignored silently. Worth knowing only so it is not mistaken for the thing that enforces www — the apex 301 and the self-referencing canonical are doing that job, and both are correct.
Exact fix
None required. Keep or remove on preference.
