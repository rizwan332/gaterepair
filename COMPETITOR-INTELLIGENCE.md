## 1. The headline number: their city pages are hollow

Everyone in this market has volume. Nobody has depth. I pulled three city pages apart:

| Page | Words | % genuinely local | Neighborhoods | Zip codes | Landmarks | Response time |
|---|---:|---:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| metrogatesrepair.com **/plano-gate-repair/** | 2,800–3,200 | **~5%** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| 4suregates.com **/gate-repair/plano/** | 2,100 | **~15–20%** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| a1gateguys.com **/dallas-electric-gate-repair/** | 850 | ~5% | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |

**Metro's Plano page** — 3,000 words, and the only Plano-specific content is the H1, the office address, and three instances of the word "Plano" inside service descriptions. Everything else — the 6-step process, all 6 FAQs, "Why choose us", brand partnerships, blog block — is byte-identical across 40+ cities. That is a find-and-replace page wearing a word count as a disguise.

**4 Sure Gates** does better, and it's not a coincidence they're the strongest organic ranker. Their local content is real but generic-affluent:
> *"Plano is one of North Texas's premier communities — known for its highly ranked neighborhoods, corporate headquarters, and well-established residential areas."*
> *"Wrought iron gates are especially popular in Plano for their durability and upscale aesthetic."*

Note what that is: a sentence that names no neighborhood, no zip, no landmark, and could be rewritten for Frisco by swapping one word. It's still 3× better than Metro.

**A1's Dallas page** has exactly one local sentence: *"Dallas is the 9th most populous city in the United States… home to DFW Airport."* Encyclopedia filler.

### What this means

**A city page with 1,200–1,800 words that is 40–50% genuinely local beats every one of these decisively** — and it's honest content, not scaled abuse. The bar is far lower than the page counts imply. See `CITY-PAGES.md` for the data model that makes this repeatable across 170 cities without it becoming a template.

## 2. Pricing — one real gap, one small player already exploiting it

I need to correct my earlier "nobody publishes pricing" — one competitor does, and it matters.

**automaticgaterepairdallas.com** publishes teaser pricing:
- *"Gate Repair – Starts at $45"*
- *"Gate Intercom Repair – Starts at $95"*
- *"10% OFF Repairs & Installations"* · *"Prices may vary"*

That's a $45 service-call hook, not real cost transparency — but it's the only pricing on any homepage in this market, and it will pull price-sensitive clicks.

**stargateandfence.com** has the only genuine cost content: a 2,100-word article giving **$2,500–$7,500** for residential automatic gate opener installation in Dallas, with this legally-careful framing repeated twice:
> *"Preliminary benchmarks for informational purposes only and do not constitute a binding quote. An on-site evaluation is required for a final proposal."*

Copy that qualifier pattern exactly. It's how you publish numbers without creating a binding quote.

**Everyone else publishes nothing.** 4 Sure: *"We quote the job before we start it."* gaterepairdallas: *"Pricing depends on the gate type, the exact problem, and whether parts are needed."* Gate Repair Pro, J&J, A-Automatic, Metro, Everlast, Garage Tec, A1: silent.

### The uncovered opportunity is **repair** pricing, not installation

Star Gate owns installation cost. **No one in DFW publishes repair price ranges.** Reference points for building it honestly:

| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| Angi (national, 2026) | Automatic gate repair **$550–$1,500**, varying by repair type and gate material |
| Star Gate (Dallas) | Gate opener **installation** $2,500–$7,500 |
| automaticgaterepairdallas.com | Service call from $45 · intercom repair from $95 |

Build a `/pricing` hub with honest bands per repair type — motor/operator, control board, sensors, keypad/remote, off-track, hinge/weld, intercom, access control — each with the Star Gate qualifier. This wins `gate repair cost dallas`, `gate opener replacement cost`, `how much to fix automatic gate` outright, and it doubles as the highest-Quality-Score landing page you can build for Ads.

## 3. Copy teardown — what actually works here

### The best-written page in the market

`stargateandfence.com/gate-opener-repair-dallas/` — 1,100 words, clean problem → agitation → solution. Opening, verbatim:

> *"Is your automatic entrance system acting up? We understand how frustrating that can be! At Star Gate & Fence, we provide professional gate opener repair for countless Dallas homeowners and businesses. When your automatic entrance stops working, it affects your daily routine and can even compromise security. Our expert team offers prompt, reliable solutions for all your automated access needs."*

Why it works: it opens on the **reader's** problem, names the emotion, then escalates the stakes from inconvenience to *security* in one sentence. That's the correct emotional ladder for this trade.

Their strongest lines:
> *"When your automatic entrance stops working, it affects your daily routine and can even compromise security."*
> *"Safety is another huge concern with DIY fixes. Automatic entrances are powerful machines that can cause injury."*
> *"Premium solutions shouldn't come with premium price tags."*

Their headings are all buyer-intent, not company-centric:
`Common Issues That Need Gate Opener Repair in Dallas` · `How We Troubleshoot…` · `Why DIY Isn't Worth It…` · `Why Choose Us…`

**Their weakness:** no FAQs on that page, no pricing, no video, almost no city pages. Shield can out-execute the best writer in the market on structure while matching them on voice.

### Best hero formula in the market

Star Gate: **`Dallas Gate Repair & Fence Installation: Same-Day Emergency Service`**
Sub: *"Secure Your Property with DFW's Top-Rated Automatic Gate Experts. Fast, Reliable, and Professional."*

Formula: **[City] + [Service] + [Urgency]**, then a subhead that leads with the *outcome* ("Secure Your Property"), not the service.

4 Sure: **`DALLAS'S TRUSTED GATE REPAIR SPECIALISTS`** / *"Automatic Gate Repair in Dallas TX You Can Count On."*
Everlast: **`Automatic Gate & Fence Installation in Dallas-Fort Worth`**

**Worst:** Metro's `Metro Gate Repair Gate Repair Dallas TX` — keyword-stuffed, brand-first, reads as spam. Dallas Automatic Gate's `Secure Entry, Smart Access` — pretty, means nothing, ranks for nothing.

### Offers in market

| Competitor | Offer |
|---|---|
| Metro Gate Repair | **$200 OFF NEW GATE OPERATOR** — "For 2 months only" |
| automaticgaterepairdallas.com | 10% off repairs & installations · $45 service call |
| Gate Repair Pro | *"no service charges for the visit — free inspection and quote"* |
| 4 Sure Gates | *"We quote the job before we start it. No surprise charges."* |
| Star Gate / Everlast / A1 | Free estimate / free consultation |

**Gap:** nobody offers a **waived service fee with repair** or a **written warranty term** as a headline offer. Both convert better than a percentage discount in emergency trades, because the objection isn't price — it's *"will they rip me off while I'm stuck?"*

## 7. Paid search landscape

- **Local Services Ads** (the Google Guaranteed pack) sit above everything and charge **per lead, $10–$70**, not per click. In an emergency trade they capture the highest-intent traffic before a single PPC ad renders. Getting Shield LSA-verified is likely the highest-ROI paid action available, and it requires the license and insurance documentation listed in `STRATEGY.md §7`.
- Texas home-services PPC is among the most competitive in the country; typical local budgets run **$1,500–$10,000+/month**.
- Only **Metro** runs an offer strong enough to headline an ad (`$200 OFF NEW GATE OPERATOR`). Most competitors bid with generic "free estimate" copy.
- **Quality Score is where Shield wins.** Ad relevance and landing page experience are two of the three QS inputs, and every competitor is pointing ads at thin, templated, slow WordPress pages. A fast Next.js page with matching H1, real photos, embedded video, published price ranges, and FAQ schema will earn a materially lower CPC than competitors bidding the same terms.
